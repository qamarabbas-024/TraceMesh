# Research & Competitive Intelligence

## Purpose

Build an evidence-based understanding of the existing ecosystem before major decisions.

## Research Scope

Research relevant competitors, established products, open-source projects, companies, workflows, technologies, architectures, design systems, pricing, business models, distribution, SEO/keywords, user complaints, reviews, security practices, performance characteristics, and known limitations.

For web products, study the actual experience where reasonably possible: onboarding, navigation, information architecture, feature hierarchy, pricing, conversion paths, retention mechanisms, empty/error states, motion, accessibility, and mobile behavior.

## Competitive Reverse Engineering

Do not merely list competitors. For each important reference product, determine:

- What it does exceptionally well
- Why users may choose it
- What users complain about
- Which workflows are strong
- Which decisions appear deliberate
- What can be learned
- What should be improved
- What should be rejected
- What should be differentiated

Never copy protected branding, proprietary content, or implementation details unlawfully. Learn from patterns and publicly observable behavior.

## Evidence Discipline

Classify important claims as:

- Verified — directly supported by reliable evidence
- Strong evidence — supported by multiple credible signals
- Reasonable inference — logically derived but not directly proven
- Assumption — currently unverified
- Unknown — evidence is insufficient

Cite sources for research-derived claims. For current information, verify freshness.

## Research the Business

When relevant, investigate pricing, monetization, target market, acquisition channels, product positioning, major keywords, distribution, free tiers, education programs, and likely cost structure.

## Student & Free Resources

Research legitimate relevant resources available to students or developers, including StudentOffers.co, GitHub Student Developer benefits, educational licenses, free tiers, grants, credits, open-source alternatives, hosting, observability, security, development, analytics, and design tools.

Do not choose a service just because it is free. Evaluate limits, reliability, privacy, security, lock-in, renewal conditions, production suitability, and what happens when the free tier ends.

## Challenge the Idea

Explicitly ask:

- Why might we not build this?
- Is the market already saturated?
- Is the proposed differentiation real?
- Is the implementation too complex for the value?
- What could make the product fail?
- What is the strongest alternative approach?

Conclude with Build / Modify / Reject recommendation and reasoning.
